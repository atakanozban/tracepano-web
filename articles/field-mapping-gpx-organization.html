Operations Workflow

Field Mapping GPX Organization in a Single Browser Panel

Field capture projects start cleanly with a handful of routes, then quickly multiply across cities, dates, and operators. Without a centralized location workflow, .gpx files and 360° street-level imagery turn into fragmented archives.

Why Field Mapping Archives Become Hard to Maintain

When field teams store GPX files on shared drives while tracking capture dates in spreadsheets, simple questions take too long to answer: Which city routes already have a .gpx track attached? How many total miles have been covered? Which routes are ready for public display versus internal review?

A 4-Step Workflow for Clean GPX Route Organization

1. Standardize Location Records

In Tracepano's admin location form, every capture entry is recorded consistently with a clear title, city, and capture date. That structure keeps historical and active field campaigns searchable in one table.

2. Attach .GPX Tracks and Set Visibility

Upload your .gpx track directly alongside the location record and choose public or private visibility from the same screen. The admin table tracks GPX status across every entry so missing files stand out immediately.

3. Find Routes Instantly with Real-Time Search

As your archive scales, real-time search filters locations without page reloads. Operators can jump straight to a city or project route, switch between routes in the left panel, and inspect synced 360° street-level panoramas with the live corner minimap.

4. Monitor Total Tracked Mileage

Instead of aggregating distances manually across individual files, Tracepano displays total tracked mileage metrics directly inside the dashboard. Combined with interactive map and satellite overlays, coordinators can verify coverage from desktop, tablet, or mobile browsers—without installing heavy desktop GIS software.
